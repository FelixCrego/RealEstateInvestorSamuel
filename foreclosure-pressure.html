Authority Guide

The Florida Foreclosure Timeline.

Florida is a judicial foreclosure state, which means the lender must go through the court system before a home can be sold at auction.

Once a Final Judgement is signed, the sale date is usually set within 30-45 days. That short window is where homeowners either protect their equity—or lose control of the outcome.

Critical Reality

Why Speed is Your Only Ally.

A traditional listing often takes 60-90 days to market, negotiate, and close—time most foreclosure cases simply do not have.

A direct cash buyer can often close in as little as 7 days, which can stop the auction in its tracks and give you a clear, documented exit before the courthouse steps decide for you.

Equity Protection

Don't Let the Bank Take Your Equity.

Many homeowners don't realize that even if they are behind on payments, they still have equity. If the bank auctions the home, that equity is often wiped out by legal fees. Selling to us now allows you to walk away with cash in your pocket to start over.

Foreclosure FAQ

Urgent answers for Florida homeowners.

Can I sell my house if I've already received a foreclosure notice?

Yes. Up until the actual auction date, you still own the home and have the legal right to sell it.

How fast can you stop the process?

We can often provide an offer in 24 hours and close within a week, providing the proof of funds your lender needs to see.

What is a 'Short Sale' vs. a 'Cash Sale'?

A cash sale is typically much faster and, when you have equity, does not require the bank's lengthy short-sale approval process.
